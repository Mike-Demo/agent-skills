# Entry curriculum

Contents: named practitioner accounts · the internal-transfer sequence, concretely · the external-entry route · certifications and why they don't gate hiring.

## Named practitioner accounts of breaking in

- **Alexis Petrichos** (Chili Piper) - entered via sales; frames the transfer explicitly as communication, negotiation and goal-alignment skills that let him structure partnership programs from scratch once he moved over.
- **Christina Cordova** - the generalist archetype: business development and partnerships combined with product-marketing work, managing engineering teams, running DEI initiatives, and hiring across growth PM and developer-advocate roles. Her path argues for breadth over a narrow partnerships specialization at the earliest stage of a company.
- **Sim Blaustein** - names the trigger for when a company creates its first dedicated partnerships seat: once founders are spending roughly 10+ hours a week responding to inbound or pursuing outbound partner requests, it makes sense to start a partnerships team. This is the internal-transfer route's precondition - someone already doing the work part-time is the default first hire.
- A Strategic Partnerships Manager, quoted in a practitioner roundup, gives the concrete internal-transfer tactic: "Put time on the right people's calendars. Ask them what they look for in a partnerships candidate." An Alliance Manager in the same piece adds the precondition: "you have to be a top performer... you'll be better served if you're at the top of your game."

## The internal-transfer route, concretely

1. Network with the partnerships team already inside the company - coffee chats, shadowing partner meetings.
2. Build a track record while still in the prior role: be the first on a sales team to fold partnerships into deal flow, showing initiative before asking for the move.
3. Build the business case with real numbers - pipeline or revenue attributable to partner-sourced work, and how partnerships shortened speed-to-market in a segment or region.
4. Ask for the move only from a position of standing.

## The external-entry route

For someone with no partnerships experience and no internal team to transfer from: target entry-level business development, account management or sales roles first. The field treats these as the transferable-skill feeder, not partnerships-specific junior roles - there is no equivalent to SDR/BDR as a dedicated first-rung partnerships title.

## Certifications - real, but non-gating

- **Partnerships Experience Academy (PXA)**, run by impact.com - the field's dominant named certification, free, tiered (fundamentals to expert), with over 50,000 professionals reported as having used it. Content covers the partnership lifecycle and the four-player affiliate/partnerships model; expert-tier content targets creators and publishers specifically, not brand-side partner managers.
- **ASAP's CA-AM and CSAP** sit on the alliance-management side specifically, cost money, and carry no independent outcome study.
- General finding: job postings consistently list these as "preferred," never "required." Practical experience and industry-network reputation outweigh any credential in this field - closer to DevRel's portfolio-driven hiring than to a licensed or exam-gated profession. Do not spend interview-prep or roadmap budget on certification-chasing.
- A general Google Analytics certification is cited as a lightweight, generically useful data-literacy signal for affiliate-manager candidates specifically, not a partnerships-specific credential.
