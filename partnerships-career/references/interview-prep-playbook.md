# Interview prep playbook

Contents: the channel-manager loop, stage by stage · named case-study themes · the judgment test and negotiation role-play · story bank · employer-diligence questions.

## Channel/Partner Manager loop, documented pattern

1. Recruiter/HR phone screen - background and fit.
2. Hiring-manager round(s), often with cross-functional stakeholders from sales, marketing and product sitting in.
3. Behavioral questions plus scenario/case-study questions on channel strategy and partner conflict.
4. Technical/tooling questions - CRM, KPIs, analytics fluency.
5. At senior levels: a panel with multiple department leaders, often asking for a 30/60/90-day channel plan or a mock partner business review. Practitioners describe this stage as feeling like a general-manager interview - judged on cross-functional leadership, not deal execution alone.

There is no standard loop length or stage set - confirm formats with the recruiter before preparing, the same caution DevRel candidates are taught.

## Named case-study/scenario themes

- **Channel conflict resolution.** A near-simultaneous deal-registration clash between a partner and direct sales is the recurring scenario. The evaluated move is process discipline over loyalty: pull the deal-registration timeline and CRM activity, bring both reps into one conversation, and treat recurrence as a symptom of unclear rules of engagement rather than a one-off to arbitrate by gut feel.
- **Cross-functional collaboration.** How do you operate simultaneously with marketing, product and sales? Strong answers name concrete mechanisms, not "I communicate well" - co-building campaigns with marketing so MDF ties to sourced pipeline, running a feedback loop that carries partner input into the product roadmap, keeping rules of engagement clean so partner and direct teams work the same number instead of colliding.
- **Partner onboarding/enablement design.** How would you evaluate a new partner (expertise, target audience, market reach, compatibility), and what materials/cadence would you ramp them on?

## The judgment test and the negotiation role-play

Prepare both explicitly - they are rehearsable and neither has an obvious "right answer" to memorize, this field's version of DevRel's opinion round.

- **"Would you close this deal in January the exact same way you closed it in December, just to finish the quarter on target?"** The rewarded answer is no - the relationship, not the quarter, is the unit a partner manager optimizes, in contrast to a quota-carrying rep for whom the quarter is the unit.
- **"Walk me through the last partner you de-prioritized, and how you had that conversation."** Tests whether you have actually said no to a partner, not just built relationships.
- **The negotiation role-play**, when included: a two-sided simulation, candidate plus an interviewer or panel playing the counterparty, in a business-partnership or vendor-deal context with hidden constraints or interests you must surface through questioning rather than being told upfront. You are judged on how you structure win-win terms, handle objections, and defend (or find) a rational walk-away point - not on whether you "won." Expect a short brief with a pressure point and one or two optional positions, not a full script; the interviewer is probing improvisation, the same way a sales mock-call probes improvisation over polish.

## What has no comparably documented pattern

Affiliate Manager, Influencer/Creator Partnerships Manager and Referral Program Manager loops are not documented with anything specific to their structure - searches return generic marketing/BD question banks, not a loop pattern tied to these titles. Prepare from the role's own outcome metrics instead:

- **Affiliate:** program performance numbers you've owned, commission-economics literacy, network/tooling fluency.
- **Influencer/Creator:** campaigns you sourced and negotiated, platform-landscape fluency, and disclosure-compliance literacy (FTC rules).
- **Referral:** incentive-design decisions you made and their measured effect, cross-team coordination examples with marketing.

## Story bank

Bank five stories before any loop, each in two lengths (two minutes, thirty seconds):

1. A partner conflict you resolved, and the process fix that followed.
2. A partnership you sourced or negotiated from nothing.
3. A partner you de-prioritized, and how that conversation went.
4. A number you moved (partner-sourced pipeline, program revenue, campaign performance) with the mechanism named, not just the count.
5. A cross-functional fight you navigated between sales, marketing and product.

## Employer diligence questions

- What is the reporting line, and what metric does that department buy (see the CEO-vs-CRO reporting question)?
- Is there a written career matrix? Can I see it?
- What's the actual attribution model for partner-sourced revenue (sourced, influenced, attached, delivered)? This field has not standardized it, and it changes what "success" means in the role.
- For a first dedicated hire: what's the current weekly time-split the founders spend on partner work today?
- For a scale-up or enterprise role: how many active partners, and is enablement centralized or fragmented across teams?
