# Posting red flags

Contents: why no named taxonomy exists · the three flagged patterns · the three questions any posting should answer.

## No field-specific taxonomy exists yet

DevRel has a named, citable framework for reading a bad posting (Joe Nash's gatekeeper/pit-trap taxonomy). Partnerships has no equivalent named taxonomy in circulation. What exists is general job-posting red-flag advice that happens to apply with unusual force to partnerships postings, because the role's boundaries - BD, relationship management, contract negotiation, marketing/sales enablement, sometimes program ops - are unusually easy to blur into one underspecified req. State this gap plainly rather than inventing a two-name taxonomy to mirror DevRel's.

## "You'll be wearing many hats"

The single most consistently flagged phrase across job-search red-flag sources, and it lands harder in partnerships than in most functions because the role already spans four adjacent disciplines before "many hats" adds anything. If a company can't clearly define the job, they likely want one person doing three roles at one salary.

**Practical test:** ask directly in the loop for a numeric time-split breakdown across the role's components (deal sourcing vs. account management vs. reporting vs. marketing collaboration). A hiring manager who answers with real numbers is describing an actual job; one who can't is describing a title in search of a job.

**Nuance:** not disqualifying at a true early-stage startup, where broad scope is the honest shape of the role (see the company-type-bar reference). The distinguishing test is whether the posting also shows undefined ownership and no stated ladder - breadth plus a stated path is a startup generalist role; breadth plus no stated path is the trap.

## Buzzword/hybrid titles

"Partnerships ninja," "growth hacker," or similar. General finding: titles like this signal the company doesn't know what it actually needs, will carry unrealistic expectations, or has an immature team culture. In partnerships specifically, this shows up as a posting collapsing partnerships, growth marketing and community-building into one hybrid title with no stated primary metric - the closest partnerships-specific echo of DevRel's "pit trap": ground-floor ownership of everything, no resources, no stated goal.

## Vague or absent compensation

"Competitive salary" with no range. Load-bearing here specifically because published benchmarks already disagree by 2-3x on the same title (see compensation-and-offer-scoring.md): a posting withholding its own number on top of unreliable public data leaves a candidate with no anchor at all. Treat an unpublished range as a stronger warning sign here than in a field with tight, trustworthy public comp data.

"Fast-paced environment" / "whatever it takes" paired with the above is a stronger overwork-culture signal than either alone, though neither is specific to this field.

## The three questions any posting should answer

1. The numeric time-split across the role's stated components.
2. The reporting line - sales, marketing, or its own function - since each measures the role on a different metric.
3. Whether a written career matrix or ladder exists at all.

A posting that can't answer any of the three is this field's closest operational proxy to a named trap.
