# Senior moves

Contents: the three routes for experienced partnerships leaders · the step-up failure modes · title-vs-mandate calibration · the advisory bridge.

## No ladder to climb, only moves to make

Partnerships has no published cross-company career ladder (see the main SKILL.md), so senior moves are lateral by default and step-ups are negotiated, not climbed. Three routes, not equally efficient:

- **efficiency:** lateral senior move > step-up into a first Head-of seat > internal promotion to VP/CPO.
- **effort:** lateral, lowest - the track record transfers directly and the loop tests what you have already done > first-Head-of step-up, moderate - you are selling a build plan, not just a record > internal promotion, highest variance - gated entirely on whether a written career matrix exists at the current employer.
- **value** (what it opens): the step-up into a first Head-of seat can skip a rung that lateral moves never will - but only when the mandate is real (team, budget, reporting line). A hollow Head-of title with no team, no budget, and a reporting line into marketing is worth less than a well-scoped Director seat.

## Route 1: the lateral senior move

Director to Director, or Head to Head, at a similar-stage company. The most common senior move in the field and the lowest-risk one: the interview loop tests the judgment test, the de-prioritization story, and the negotiation role-play against a record you already hold, so preparation is story-bank work rather than new-skill work (see [interview-prep-playbook.md](./interview-prep-playbook.md)).

Watch for title drift between companies: because titles are unstandardized, confirm the mandate (team size, budget, decision rights) matches the title band you are leaving, not just the title string.

## Route 2: the step-up into a first Head-of seat

The scale-up or startup hiring its first partnerships leader. This is the only route to a Head-of title at companies with no existing partnerships leadership, and the highest-leverage move available - a successful build puts you in the VP conversation two years sooner than a lateral would.

Two candidate-side failure modes, mirroring the hiring-side ones in [company-type-bar.md](./company-type-bar.md):

- **Too senior for the actual work.** Expecting to set strategy and hire a team when the first two quarters are hands-on pipeline building you do yourself. Before accepting, get the founder to describe what the Head of Partnerships does in a typical week in month two - if the answer is all strategy and hiring, the mandate is underspecified.
- **No stated mandate.** Accepting "Head of" with no team, no budget, and a reporting line into marketing or sales ops. The employer-diligence questions in [interview-prep-playbook.md](./interview-prep-playbook.md) exist specifically to smoke this out before an offer: reporting line and its metric, headcount and budget ownership, the attribution model behind any variable comp.

A third, quieter risk: the **step-up that is actually a step sideways in comp**. First-Head-of seats at early startups frequently trade title for below-market cash. Price the trade explicitly against your floor before the loop starts, not after the offer lands.

## Route 3: internal promotion to VP/CPO

Pursueable only where a written career matrix exists - ask for it first. Without one, the "promotion" is a title change with the same mandate; negotiate the mandate (team, budget, comp, decision rights) instead of the title. The field's own two-tier split is the leverage here: a VP is still measured on program delivery, a CPO sets company strategy - a promotion worthy of the name moves you across that line, not just up a title string.

## The advisory/consulting bridge

Fractional or advisory partnerships work while searching: keeps the track record warm, expands the relationship surface that produces the next full-time seat, and can convert into the seat itself. Two cautions: it does not replace a full-time seat for comp benchmarking (advisory rates and salary bands are different populations - never quote one against the other), and a long advisory stretch without a full-time anchor can read as "consultant, not operator" to hiring managers screening for the step-up route. Time-box it.

## Title-vs-mandate calibration

Because the field never standardized titles, calibrate every opportunity on the mandate/decides columns in [role-taxonomy-and-skills.md](./role-taxonomy-and-skills.md), never on the title alone:

- A "Head of Partnerships" at a 20-person startup can carry less scope than a Senior Partner Manager at an enterprise.
- A "VP Partnerships" reporting to a CMO with no team is a partnerships-marketing seat wearing a VP title; the same title reporting to the CEO with P&L ownership is the real one.
- Ask for the numeric time-split, the reporting line, and the written career matrix (the three posting questions in [posting-red-flags.md](./posting-red-flags.md)) - at senior level these are mandate-diligence questions, and a company that cannot answer them is telling you the mandate does not exist yet.
