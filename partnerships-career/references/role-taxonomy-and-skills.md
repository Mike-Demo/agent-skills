# Role taxonomy and skills

Contents: the four sub-disciplines in detail · hard and soft skills, junior vs senior · the ladder finding.

## Affiliate Manager

Progression: Affiliate Coordinator -> Affiliate Manager -> Senior Affiliate Manager -> Director of Partnerships -> VP of Affiliate Marketing.

Screened on: demonstrable experience running affiliate programs or performance marketing, web-analytics/data literacy, familiarity with affiliate-network tooling. A marketing or business degree helps at the first job and stops mattering once a track record exists.

The level-defining shift is reporting scope, not tenure: coordinators report activity (clicks, conversions, commissions paid); directors report outcomes (revenue contribution, CAC efficiency, incrementality, channel ROI against paid media). A candidate who can only talk in click/conversion terms reads as capped below director regardless of years in seat. Typical time to mid-level: 4-6 years of good performance at a growing operator - the step into people management is the steep one, since lower levels rarely build management reps on the job.

## Influencer/Creator Partnerships Manager

Almost never a direct point of entry: a specialist track built on top of broader marketing or social-media experience, not an entry-level hire in its own right. Candidates arrive via adjacent roles (social media, brand partnerships, account management, digital marketing) more often than a dedicated junior influencer track.

Rough progression: entry-level social/digital marketing (foundation) -> specialist, 2-4 years, owns outreach/execution/reporting on a channel -> manager, 2-4 years, owns end-to-end campaigns: sourcing, negotiation, timeline, performance -> senior/leadership, broader strategy, team management, cross-functional executive exposure, budget influence.

Screened on: communication and negotiation skill, working knowledge of the current platform/creator landscape, analytics tool fluency, and - increasingly load-bearing - FTC/advertising-disclosure compliance literacy for the campaigns run. Postings commonly ask for roughly 4 years in marketing or user-growth roles inside tech or entertainment for the manager-level seat, not a fixed credential.

## Referral Program Manager

Two unrelated occupations share this title. Disambiguate which one a posting means before anything else applies:

- **Customer/growth referral** - owns refer-a-friend program design, incentive structure, tracking, and CRM-side coordination with marketing.
- **Employee-referral recruiting** - an HR/TA function: internal referral programs for hiring, works with HR and hiring managers, tracks referral-to-hire conversion.

Screened on, for the customer/growth track: program strategy and incentive design, data analysis of referral performance, cross-team coordination with marketing and product, CRM/tracking-platform fluency. No published multi-rung ladder exists for this title; it tends to sit as one IC role inside a growth or lifecycle-marketing team rather than anchoring its own career track. The honest next move from this seat is usually lateral into a broader referral-plus-affiliate-plus-loyalty remit, or into a Partner Marketing lane, not a same-title promotion.

## Channel/Alliance Manager and the strategy-level roles

| Role                                       | Mandate                                                                                                                                | Decides                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Partner/Channel Manager (CAM/PAM)          | Day-to-day portfolio: recruiting, onboarding, enablement, deal registration, satisfaction                                              | Which deals to support, partner-level plans       |
| Alliance Lead                              | A handful of strategically consequential partnerships (GSIs, hyperscalers, tech alliances); co-sell, co-innovation, exec relationships | Joint GTM motions, joint solution investment      |
| Head of Partnerships / CPO / Channel Chief | Strategy, business planning, operating cadence, attribution reporting, headcount and budget                                            | Program strategy, tiering, budget, partner types  |
| Partner Marketing                          | JVPs, co-marketing, through-channel marketing, MDF-funded activity                                                                     | Campaign design, MDF allocation to marketing      |
| Partner Operations                         | Onboarding workflows, reporting, PRM admin, attribution, integrations                                                                  | Tooling config, data/attribution rules            |
| Partner Enablement Manager                 | Partner onboarding design, role-based learning tracks, certification, content curation                                                 | Curriculum, certification design, refresh cadence |

Sharpest functional distinction: a channel manager is measured on pipeline volume and deal-registration counts across a broad population; an alliance manager on depth of executive engagement and co-sell revenue across a few large partners.

## Hard and soft skills, junior vs senior (applies across the business-partner side of the field)

| Dimension                        | Junior/entry signal                                                   | Senior signal                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Relationship read                | Understands the partner org chart - who signs, who blocks             | Also maps motivations and stakes across the partner's org, not just its structure                                 |
| Reporting                        | Reports activity: partners recruited, deals registered, campaigns run | Reports impact: partner-sourced revenue, ACV contribution, retention, CAC or incrementality versus other channels |
| Focus under competing priorities | Executes one motion at a time                                         | Holds partner, customer and internal sales process in view simultaneously without losing the thread               |
| Deal orientation                 | Optimizes to close inside the current period                          | Treats the relationship, not the quarter, as the unit of measurement                                              |
| Judgment on de-prioritization    | Rarely has said no to a partner                                       | Can walk through a specific partner de-prioritized, why, and how that conversation went                           |

## The ladder finding

Unlike sales (Bridge Group's published SDR-to-AE benchmarks) or DevRel (a documented six-rung IC ladder scored on five dimensions), partnerships has no equivalent cross-company published career ladder.

- Title proliferation with no standardization - LinkedIn alone carries roughly 142,000 "Channel," 133,000 "Partnerships" and 23,000 "Alliances" titles, so the same seniority band can carry very different titles at different companies.
- The one formally recognized senior tier is the Chief Partner Officer / VP Partner Ecosystem role - the fastest-growing C-level title tracked in the space, but a single top-of-ladder marker, not a rung-by-rung framework.
- Practitioners distinguish a VP (an execution role, still measured on program delivery) from a CPO/C-level role (sets company strategy) - the closest thing to a two-tier leadership split the field publishes.

Ask a target employer for a written career matrix first. When none exists, use the mandate/decides table above as the closest available level differentiator.
